About the PDE, the model and its limits

Equation

∂tu + ∂x(u²/2) = (ν/π) ∂xxu, x ∈ [0,1) periodic, t ∈ [0, 2]. The nonlinear term steepens waves into fronts; viscosity smooths them. This is the PDEBench convention (diffusion coefficient ν/π), which we verified by reproducing PDEBench trajectories with our own solver.

Model

A Fourier Neural Operator maps u(x,0) on a 256-point grid directly to u(x,t) at t = 0.2, 0.4, …, 2.0 (10 snapshots), conserving the spatial mean exactly. It was trained on a subset of the official PDEBench 1D Burgers data for a single viscosity.

Validation

PDEBench test samples are compared with the true held-out PDEBench solution. Your own inputs have no stored answer, so they are compared with an independent pseudo-spectral solver (exact diffusion, RK4, 2/3 dealiasing). It was validated against 30 stored PDEBench trajectories at ν = 0.1: mean relative L2 ≈ 1×10−3, max absolute error ≤ 5×10−3 (the alternative equation form D = ν is ~130× worse, so the PDE convention is confirmed).

Limitations

  • Only the trained viscosity values are accepted; others are rejected, not extrapolated.
  • Input must be on the model grid (N points, periodic). Amplitudes above the training range are rejected.
  • Initial conditions unlike the training distribution (sharp or high-frequency) are accepted with a warning; the FNO error can then be large, so check the solver comparison.
  • At ν = 0.1 about 96% of the fluctuation energy has dissipated by t = 0.5, so late-time fields are nearly constant. Relative errors on the remaining small fluctuations are large even for the reference solver (~7% at t = 2 vs PDEBench), so late-time comparisons have a floor of a few percent.
  • Outputs exist only at the trained snapshot times. The model knows nothing about other boundary conditions or domains.
